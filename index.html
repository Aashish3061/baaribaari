The problem

Your parent lives in another city. So who handles the calls, the bookings and the paperwork?

In many families, nobody decides. It just lands on one sibling, and raising it feels like starting an argument.

Distance changes what caring looks like, but remote work still counts. Booking the specialist from Bengaluru, sorting the pension forms on a Sunday night, the weekly video call: it's all care, even if no one sees it.

How it works

Three minutes from silence to a starting point.

Describe your family

How many siblings, where each lives, the time and money each can offer, and how each likes to help. No names needed.

Tick what your parent needs

Visits, calls, bookings, bills and paperwork, groceries, repairs. Never medicines or health decisions.

Get a draft, then talk

Who owns what, a cost split worked out in code, and a WhatsApp message in English, Hinglish or Hindi. Edit it and send it.

Principles

A starting proposal, never a verdict.

No scorecards

We don't rate siblings or call anyone unfair. Remote work is credited alongside who lives nearby.

No medical advice

BaariBaari plans who does what. Medicines, symptoms and doses belong with your parent's doctor. In an emergency in India, call 112.

No names stored

Siblings are A, B, C, D. Your free-text note is used once and never saved. Arithmetic like cost shares is done in code, not by the AI.

Questions

Good to know

Is this a full app?

Not yet. This is an early, free web demo of the core idea: getting from "who does what?" to a draft everyone can react to.

What do you store?

The options you pick (cities as categories, time and money bands, needs, language), the AI's reply and token counts, so we can show the live numbers above. We never store names, and we never store your free-text note.

How is the cost split worked out?

In code, from the money band each sibling chose (none, some, a larger share), rounded so it always adds up to 100%. The AI only writes the words around it.

Can it help with medicines or symptoms?

No, by design. For health questions, please speak to your parent's doctor. For non-emergency support for older persons, India's national Elderline is 14567; for emergencies, call 112.
